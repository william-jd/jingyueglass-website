Borosilicate vs. Soda-Lime Glass: Which Is Best for Your Drinkware Line?

By Jingyue Glass Engineering Team • October 2026 • 5 Min Read
⚡ Quick Answer: Borosilicate vs. Soda-Lime Glass Key Takeaways

Borosilicate Glass (COE 3.3) excels in extreme thermal shock resistance (-20°C to 150°C), making it ideal for boiling coffee, iced tea, and double-wall insulated mugs. Soda-Lime Glass has higher density and mechanical hardness, making it ideal for heavy-bottomed whiskey tumblers, beer steins, and cost-effective high-volume bar glassware.

1. Technical Properties Comparison

Engineering Metric Borosilicate Glass 3.3 Commercial Soda-Lime Glass
Thermal Expansion (COE) 3.3 × 10⁻⁶ / K (Low expansion) 8.5–9.0 × 10⁻⁶ / K (Standard)
Thermal Shock Tolerance ΔT = 150°C (Freezer to Boiling) ΔT = 50°C–60°C (Requires gradual tempering)
Density & Weight 2.23 g/cm³ (Lightweight feel) 2.52 g/cm³ (Substantial, weighted feel)
Primary B2B Product Categories Double-wall mugs, teapots, floral cups Whiskey tumblers, beer steins, highball glasses

2. Sourcing Recommendations for Importers

Choose Borosilicate Glass if you are creating modern coffee ware, thermal mugs, or lifestyle gift sets that undergo microwave or boiling water use. Choose Soda-Lime or Lead-Free Crystal if you need tactile weight, heavy bottoms, or commercial durability for hospitality venues.
