Why Do Whiskey Glasses Have Heavy Bases? The Science of Thermal Mass & Balance

By Jingyue Glass R&D Team • October 2026 • 5 Min Read
⚡ Quick Answer: Why Whiskey Glasses Feature a Heavy Base (Sham)

Whiskey glasses feature a solid heavy base (known as the sham) for 3 primary reasons: 1) Lowering the center of gravity to resist accidental bar tipping; 2) Hand heat shielding that slows ice melt rate by up to 40%; and 3) Impact resistance to withstand downward cocktail muddling forces without stress fracturing.

1. Thermal Insulation and Retarding Ice Dilution

When serving premium bourbon, scotch, or whiskey over a large ice sphere, hand contact is the greatest threat to beverage temperature. A solid 15mm to 25mm glass base acts as an insulating thermal buffer. Drinkers naturally grip the vessel around the heavy sham, isolating warm fingers from the liquor bowl and preserving chilled sipping profiles without excessive water dilution.

2. Physics of Rotational Inertia and Bar Stability

In bustling hospitality settings, drinkware tipping causes significant inventory loss and safety hazards. A heavy base concentrates more than 55% of the total vessel weight in the lower third. This dramatically increases rotational stability, ensuring the glass stays upright even when bumped on crowded bar counters or bussing trays.

3. Mechanical Strength for Muddling & Bar Operations

Traditional cocktails such as the Old Fashioned require muddling citrus peels and sugar directly in the glass. A reinforced heavy bottom provides a solid anvil surface that withstands high-pressure muddling tools without micro-cracking or base separation.
